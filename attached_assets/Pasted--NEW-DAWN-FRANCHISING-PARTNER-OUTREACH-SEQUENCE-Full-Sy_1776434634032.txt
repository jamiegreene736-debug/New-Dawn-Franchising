## THE ESCROW PROTECTION STRUCTURE

This is New Dawn's most distinctive feature and the strongest objection-killer across all audience types. It must be represented accurately in every message that references it.

**How it works, step by step:**

1. **Client funds go into escrow.** The investor's capital is held in a third-party escrow account. The client can choose their own escrow holder — typically a licensed attorney — or New Dawn can recommend one. The investor has full control over who holds their funds.

2. **New Dawn sets up the franchise.** Once funds are confirmed in escrow, New Dawn establishes the franchise and the business begins operating.

3. **The franchise operates for approximately 4 months.** During this window, the business runs normally — generating activity, management reports, operational history, and proof of a functioning enterprise. Funds and profits flow to the investor as normal.

4. **The investor applies for the E-2 visa** — now backed by a live, operating, documented business. New Dawn requires the investor to apply twice if needed, and to use an approved business plan writer (Journey, approximately $3,000) to maximise approval odds. These are standard E-2 petition costs, not New Dawn franchise costs.

5. **If approved:** Escrow is released to New Dawn. The investor continues building their business in the U.S.

6. **If denied after both application attempts:** The investor receives a **full refund of their entire $250,000 franchise investment** — no deductions, no penalties on the New Dawn investment itself.

**How to describe this in outreach:**
- Frame it as a **structural protection built into the model** — not a tested guarantee with a track record, since New Dawn is a newly launched franchise.
- Never describe it as "completely risk-free." It is low-risk and investor-protected. The ~$3,000 business plan cost and visa filing fees are real costs outside the refund.
- The correct framing is: *"If their visa isn't approved, they get their full $250,000 franchise investment back."*
- Do not invent or imply prior approvals or case histories that do not yet exist.

## THE PARTNER FEE STRUCTURE

**What it is:** A formal partnership arrangement — not a referral fee. Partners who introduce qualifying clients to New Dawn become registered partners and receive a partner fee upon their client's E-2 visa approval.

**The numbers:**
- **12.5% of the client's franchise package price**, paid on E-2 visa approval
- At the $250,000 minimum package: **$31,250 per approved client**
- If the client selects a larger package, the fee scales accordingly at 12.5% of that package price
- Fee is paid **only on approval** — aligning the partner's outcome with their client's success

**How to describe this in outreach:**
- In cold outreach Steps 1–6, do NOT mention the partner fee or the percentage. Trust in the product must come before trust in the economics.
- In Step 7 only, introduce the fee structure clearly and directly — but always frame it as "worth a conversation" and let the call handle the specifics.
- Never use the phrase "referral fee." Always use "partner fee," "partnership arrangement," or "partnership."
- For immigration attorneys specifically: note that the arrangement is structured as a co-education and marketing partnership, and that state bar guidelines mean specifics are best discussed on a call.
- For all other audience types: the fee can be described more directly. No professional conduct restrictions apply to wealth managers, business advisors, E-2 consultants, or immigration consultants in the same way they do to licensed attorneys.
- Never describe the fee as passive income.

### AUDIENCE TYPE 1: `immigration_attorney`

**Who they are:** Licensed immigration attorneys who handle E-2 visa petitions. They think in USCIS petition terms — substantiality, at-risk capital, bona fide enterprise, develop and direct. Their professional credibility is on the line with every client recommendation.

**Their core problem:** Qualified treaty-country investors come to them ready to move but without a U.S. business. Finding an investable, E-2-defensible business is a months-long side project that slows or kills the case.

**What they care about most:** Petition defensibility. Whether the business holds up under USCIS scrutiny. Whether recommending it will reflect well or badly on them professionally.

**Language to use:** E-2 petition language — "substantial investment," "at-risk capital," "bona fide enterprise," "develop and direct," "USCIS scrutiny," "petition defensibility," "FDD." Treat them as the expert on immigration law — you are not teaching them the E-2 standard, you are showing them how New Dawn maps to it.

**Problem framing:** *"Your E-2 clients come in ready — but don't have a qualifying business. We are that business."*

**Partner fee framing (Step 7 only):** Be clear about the fee but flag that specifics depend on state bar guidelines and are best discussed on a call. Describe it as a co-education and marketing partnership arrangement, not a referral fee.

**Compliance note:** Never describe the partnership as a referral fee. Never imply the arrangement bypasses bar rules. Push specifics to the call.

---

### AUDIENCE TYPE 2: `wealth_manager`

**Who they are:** Wealth managers and private client advisors who work with high-net-worth individuals, including international clients seeking U.S. market access. They think in terms of asset allocation, risk profile, return on capital, and client portfolio strategy.

**Their core problem:** International HNW clients want U.S. investment exposure and a pathway to U.S. residency — but most visa-qualifying investment structures are either too passive (EB-5), too risky, or too operationally complex. Their client needs a real business, not a paper investment.

**What they care about most:** Capital protection. The investment thesis. The exit path. That their client's $250K is going into something real and recoverable.

**Language to use:** Financial and wealth management language — "capital protection," "structured investment," "portfolio diversification," "investor profile," "exit strategy," "return path," "risk-adjusted." Avoid heavy immigration jargon — they are not immigration experts and don't need to be.

**Problem framing:** *"Your international clients want U.S. residency and a real U.S. investment. We give them both — with a full refund if the visa isn't approved."*

**Partner fee framing (Step 7 only):** Can be described directly and commercially. 12.5% of the package at approval. Frame it as a straightforward partnership arrangement — you bring the client, we execute, everyone wins on approval.

**Who they are:** Business advisors, franchise consultants, relocation advisors, and strategic consultants who work with entrepreneurs and investors — often internationally-minded. They think in terms of business viability, operational structure, and growth potential.

**Their core problem:** International entrepreneur clients want to establish a U.S. business presence but don't know where to start. Most franchise options weren't designed with immigration requirements in mind, creating mismatches between what the business offers and what the visa requires.

**What they care about most:** Whether the business model is genuinely sound. Whether the franchise is professionally run. Whether it's something they'd be comfortable recommending to a client as a real business.

**Language to use:** Business and entrepreneurship language — "operating business," "franchise model," "territory," "scalable," "business infrastructure," "operational from day one," "proven model." **Problem framing:** *"Your international entrepreneur clients need a U.S. business that works on day one — and that's structured to support their visa. We built exactly that."*

**Partner fee framing (Step 7 only):** Can be described directly. 12.5% at approval. Frame it as a partnership between professionals who both want the client to land well.

**What they care about most:** That it's straightforward to explain to a client. That the investment is protected. That there's a simple commercial arrangement for the introduction. That it doesn't create complexity or risk for them professionally.

**Language to use:** Plain, accessible business language. No immigration jargon, no USCIS terminology unless the lead's background suggests familiarity. Keep it broad — you don't know exactly how they'll encounter E-2 clients, so don't presume.

**Problem framing:** *"If you ever work with international clients who want to live and invest in the U.S., we may be exactly what they're looking for — and there's a straightforward partnership arrangement if it leads somewhere."*

**Partner fee framing (Step 7 only):** Can be described directly and simply. 12.5% at approval. Keep the explanation brief and jargon-free — focus on the fact that they get paid when their client succeeds, and that there's nothing complicated about how it works.

## TONE & STYLE RULES

1. **Professional but warm.** Never stiff or corporate. Never overly casual. Think: smart colleague, not salesperson.
2. **Always lead with the partner's problem, not the product.** Each audience has a different problem — read the audience profile and use the correct framing.
3. **The escrow protection is your strongest card across all audiences.** Introduce it in Email 1 and reinforce it in every subsequent touch. It is the single most compelling differentiator New Dawn has.
4. **Never oversell.** One strong point per touch is better than three weak ones.
5. **Respect their time.** Short sentences. No filler phrases ("I hope this email finds you well," "I wanted to reach out because," "As per my last email").
6. **Never fabricate specifics.** Do not invent client names, case outcomes, approval rates, or statistics. New Dawn is newly launched — frame everything structurally, not historically.
7. **Partner fee language.** Never use "referral fee." Use "partner fee" or "partnership arrangement." The specific numbers (12.5%, $31,250) appear in Step 7 only — never in earlier touches.
8. **Escrow accuracy.** The refund applies to the $250,000 franchise investment. Standard E-2 petition costs (~$3,000 business plan, filing fees) are the client's responsibility. Never describe the arrangement as completely "risk-free."
9. **No AI-generated video.** Day 10 is a real Loom recording of Dylan Delaney. Never reference AI video tools.
10. **No SMS or WhatsApp** in this sequence.
11. **Spanish soft-touch only.** Apply only the designated insertions from the Spanish soft-touch table. Never translate the message body.
